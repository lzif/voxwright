## Build observed

A survival base cut into a rolling hillside around a rectangular quarry pit with a waterfall. On the north and west rim: a half-timber lodge (stone ground floor, timber upper, brick-red roof with timber ribs, cross-gable dormer, balcony), a lean-to wing, a windmill tower and an ore-crushing shed. In the pit: benches stepping down every wall with straight stone stairs cut in, an amethyst-mine tunnel mouth, three timber bridge levels with minecart rails, wall-support beams, two gantry cranes with pulley and chain, and a flooded floor with lily pads (q01, q02, q04, q06, q07). A stream runs through a stone-lined tunnel under the lodge, passes a plank footbridge and drops over the rim as the waterfall (q01, q02, q03).

## Terminology (one term per element)

- **Quarry pit:** the rectangular excavation cut into the hill, with benches down every wall.
- **Bench:** a 1–3-block ledge in the pit wall, reached by a straight stair cut into the wall.
- **Rim gradient:** the three interleaving bands at the pit edge: light-grey stone, orange-brown dirt patches, grass.
- **Wall support beam:** a 1-wide timber post standing 1–2 blocks off the rock face, tied back to the rock by a beam at the top.
- **Trestle bridge:** a timber crossing on paired posts with a cap beam, diagonal braces under the deck and minecart rails on top.

## Candidates

### 1. Benched excavation pit — PROPOSED NEW

**Status: proposed NEW (stage 1, unroasted). Confidence MED** (the structure is clear in five stills; every dimension is unresolved). **Band: mega** (technique 9's band; the pit is the build's anchor and about a lodge-plus-yard in size).

- **Principle:** technique 9 says to anchor a build on a terrain feature ("quarry pit, waterfall, flooded basin") but gives no construction for the feature itself. A quarry pit that is just a rectangular hole reads as a missing chunk of the world; this one reads as worked ground because the walls are benched, jittered and layered, the rim blends outward, and timber and stairs give the hole its scale. The pit is subtractive terrain: the construction is the cut.
- **Recipe (OBSERVED unless tagged; q01, q02, q04, q06, q07):**
  1. Cut a rectangular pit with an irregular rim (q07), roughly a lodge-plus-yard in plan.
  2. Step every wall down as benches, 1–3-block ledges (D's read, not counted), not a funnel; run a straight stone staircase cut into the wall along them (q06 "Staircase #3", q04 lower left, q01).
  3. Jitter the wall faces and the rim in and out by ~1–3 blocks so no face is planar (q04, q06).
  4. Show strata at the outer cut face: grass, then a brown dirt band, then stone (q02 right side).
  5. Blend the rim in three interleaving bands outward, light-grey stone, orange-brown dirt patches, grass, with noisy, interleaved boundaries (q07, q01).
  6. Mix the rock from four or five stones with moss and vine patches on lower walls and ledges (q04, q02).
  7. Tie timber to the rock: 1-wide wall support beams 1–2 off the face with a top beam back to the rock (q04, q02); a timber-framed tunnel mouth cut into a wall (q06); bridge portal posts resting on ledges (q04, q02).
  8. Give the floor a function: flooded with lily pads (q02, q04), or stock and ore at the rim (stacked cut cubes, a cart, crates; q01 bottom left, q06).
- **Apply when:** a survival base, quarry or mine diorama whose anchor is an extraction pit (technique 9's terrain-feature anchor). **NOT when:** the feature is a hill the compound wraps (technique 9's inverted orbit verb: wrap the hill and cut the entrance into it); a pit with no stairs, bridge, stock or water on it, which reads as a hole (the scale objects are part of the recipe); builds on flat, featureless sites where the cut has no hill to show strata against (INFERRED guard, for the roast).
- **Prevents:** the "rectangular hole" read, the "funnel" read (one slope from rim to floor), and a hole with a clean rim that sits on the terrain like a stencil.
- **Unresolved:** ALL dimensions (pit size and depth, bench count, height and depth, waterfall drop, bridge spans); the exact rock mix; whether the pink cast on the stone is the shader or the blocks.
- **Cross-refs:** the hibouk pool pit is a small instance of steps 1–2 (`hibouk-winter-mansion`, observations). T1's "weather by environment" already covers the moss-at-the-waterline band; T5's "hang vegetation on tall old faces" covers the vines.
- (Dio ref: q07 overview and rim; q01, q02 pit from two sides; q04 benches, jitter, support beams, stairs; q06 stair, tunnel mouth and rim stock.)

### 2. Rim gradient — stone, dirt patches, grass — PROPOSED T5 SUB-ENTRY

**Status: proposed sub-entry of technique 5 ("Sit it in the terrain"; stage 1, unroasted). Confidence MED.** **Band: mini** (any cut or footing edge).

- **Principle:** technique 5 asks for patchy paths and plants around the base, not a clean cutout on flat grass. Here the same logic is applied to an excavation edge: the transition from cut ground to grass is three interleaving bands rather than one line (q07, q01).
- **Recipe (OBSERVED):** at the pit rim, a ring of light-grey stone, then orange-brown coarse-dirt patches, then grass, with every boundary noisy and bands interleaving; never a straight cut edge. The lodge's stone ground floor runs flush to the rim and the cut face (q01, q03).
- **Apply when:** any cut or dug edge (pit, pool, terrace cut, quarry). **NOT when:** a formal edge language is intended (technique 8's FORMAL water: raised stone rim, chamfered corners).
- (Dio ref: q07, q01.)
