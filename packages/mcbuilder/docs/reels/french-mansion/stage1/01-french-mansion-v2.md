## NEW candidates (added by the dense set — v1's 8 frames missed these)

### 6. Sacrificial footprint outline in a contrasting block (NEW — MED-HIGH)
- **Principle:** Before any wall goes up, the whole footprint is drawn on the
  grass in a contrasting block, then progressively replaced by the real
  foundation as construction proceeds. The outline is a layout tool, not a
  material — it is consumed by the build.
- **Recipe:** Lay the full footprint outline 1 block wide in a high-contrast
  sacrificial block (here: gold blocks on green grass — any block that reads
  against the terrain works). Build the stone foundation over it section by
  section, replacing the outline as you go; the last outline segments vanish
  when the floor slab fills. Keep the outline 1 wide and exactly on the
  foundation line — a 2-wide or offset outline confuses the wall line.
- **Apply when:** any large build where the footprint shape is non-trivial
  (wings, projections, courtyards) — the outline is what keeps the wings
  aligned during the first courses. **NOT when:** small builds (a 9×9 cottage
  does not need a layout pass — T2's skeleton-first covers it).
- **Prevents:** wing drift — without a drawn outline, long builds accumulate
  1-block alignment errors between wings that only show up at roof height.
- **Refs:** f0001–f0012 (gold outline → stone replacement → slab fill, in
  sequence). **Resolves v1's unresolved read:** the "gold segment" is NOT a
  material — it is the sacrificial layout line mid-replacement.
- **Convergence note for stage 3:** the Japanese-house reel's pending
  "compound site-plan marking" candidate is the same outline-first family
  (zone lines on grass). Stage 3 should merge these into ONE outline-first
  technique with two recipes (compound zone marking vs sacrificial footprint
  outline) rather than numbering two separate techniques.

### 7. Parterre cross + circle node garden (NEW — MED, roast to decide)
- **Principle:** T8's formal grounds are separate enclosed plots. This garden is
  ONE room divided by a path cross with a circular node at the crossing:
  four quadrant beds around a central circle, the whole room edged by a
  balustrade + topiary row. The cross + circle is the composition — the beds
  are subordinate to it, not independent plots.
- **Recipe:** One rectangular garden room, balustrade + topiary-row edge. Two
  paths crossing at the room's center (gravel/stone), a circular lawn node
  (5–7 diameter) at the crossing, four quadrant beds (lawn centers with
  dirt/flower borders) in the corners. Keep the circle as lawn, not a
  fountain — the fountain court is a separate garden room (see below).
- **Apply when:** formal estates with room for a second garden room beside the
  fountain court. **NOT when:** small grounds (the cross + circle needs
  ≥15×15 to read — smaller, it is just paths); when the fountain court already
  occupies the front axis (one axial garden room per axis — this one goes on
  the side axis).
- **Refs:** f0117 (full aerial: cross paths, central circular lawn, quadrant
  beds, balustrade + topiary edge). Single-room read from one aerial angle —
  hence MED, and the roast must decide standalone vs T8 extension.
- **Prevents:** the "paths to nowhere" formal garden — beds without a
  circulation figure read as separate plots; the cross + circle gives the
  garden a walkable diagram.
