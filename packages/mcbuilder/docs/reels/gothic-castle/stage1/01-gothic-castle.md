## Fidelity caveat: this video is an edited composite, not a faithful chronological timelapse

Four facts force this caveat, and every order claim in both files is read through it:

1. **Floating chandeliers (f_002/f_003).** Two chandelier clusters hang from chains that exit the top of the frame, above nave walls that are only ~3-4 tall, with no roof, beam, or any support visible anywhere. A chandelier cannot be built hanging from nothing. Its appearance this early is an edit artifact, not a build step.
2. **The tower crown disagrees between showcase frames.** In f_013 the tall left tower is flat-topped with small pinnacles around the rim and no spire. In f_014 and f_015 the same tower (same rose, same dark panel, same upper story with brown windows) carries a tall dark spire with a cross finial. A spire cannot appear and disappear between showcase frames of one build state.
3. **The tower upper story appears late.** In f_007 the rose sits directly below the spire with no story between them. In f_013-f_015 a full upper story (~8-10 tall, brown glazed arched windows, two corner turrets) stands between the rose and the spire. The camera never shows the main tower's front face between f_007 and f_013, so the story's build time is unobserved, not proven late.
4. **The panel below the rose disagrees between frames.** In f_012 the panel below the rose is flat dark grey with no grid visible. In f_013 and f_014 the same panel is gridded: 1 vertical bar plus 7-8 horizontal bars, with brownish-grey glass between them. A gridded panel versus a flat panel is a difference of content, not of lighting: at the same scale the grid in f_013/f_014 reads clearly while no grid exists in f_012. This is a fourth piece of evidence that the video is composite.

Consequence: first-appearance order in this reel is a reconstruction with low confidence for the tower crown and the chandeliers. Where the video's apparent order conflicts with a sane build order, the files below give the sane order and say so. No process rule is derived from the chandelier timing or the tower crown timing (see 02, verdict).

## Terminology (one term per element)

- **Rose window:** a circular dark-tracery window, ~4 diameter, near the top of a tall arched opening, with glass or a dark panel below it. Two constructions exist in this reel (candidate 1, variants A and B). NOT a window punched in a wall in variant A: the wall IS the window's frame.
- **Tracery:** the dark pattern inside a window (rose circle, mullion grid). Material UNRESOLVED (iron bars or fences read the same at this resolution; the crop shows a thick, dense dark pattern, not thin iron-bar rods). Never asserted as iron bars.
- **Mullion:** a dark bar dividing the glass, vertical or horizontal. A nave window's mullion grid is 1 vertical bar (~10 tall) + 5-7 horizontal bars (counted in zoom, f_003/f_004, crop-rose-window.jpg), so ~25-31 tracery pieces per ~3x10 window. The old "2-3 horizontal" is withdrawn.
- **Bay:** one wall section between two pilasters: one window plus its flanking piers. The nave repeats one bay recipe along its side.
- **Pilaster strip (engaged pier):** a 1-wide vertical strip on a wall face, flush or ~1 proud, with a wider plinth at the base and a cap course at the top. The "buttresses" in this reel are straight pilasters, NOT stepped buttresses (no stepping in 1 block per ~4 rise is visible in f_003/f_004).
- **Pinnacle:** a small spire (1-2 wide, ~3-5 tall) sitting on a parapet, buttress cap, or ridge end, a miniature of the main spire, never a full tower, no windows.
- **Turret:** a small tower with windows, rising from a corner or mid-height to above the roofline (f_007/f_013/f_014: corner turrets with small windows flank the tower crown). A turret is NOT a pinnacle; the two are different elements.
- **Cross finial:** a spire tip as a thin rod with crossbars, not a plain spike.
- **Ridge spike:** a 1-block finial on a ridge between pinnacles.
- **Cap course:** the proud course topping a pier, turret, or tower stage.
- **Setback:** a step inward of the wall face at a stage transition (the tower narrows at the upper story).
- **Belfry-like story:** the top story of a tower with arched openings. Here it has brown glazed arched windows and NO bell is visible in any frame, so it is called belfry-like, not a belfry (T25 requires a visible bell).
- **White frieze:** the pale trim course (blocks or slabs) running under the eaves and above arched openings, reading as a dentil/scallop row in f_008/f_009/f_012. NOT a beam course, it carries no structure in the read. Already covered by T1 two-tone edges, not a candidate.

## Candidates

### 1. Rose window as tracery wall (FOLD-READY, NEW)

**Status: fold-ready.** **Band: mega** (needs a face ~6 wide x ~12 tall minimum for a rose ~4 diameter; below ~6 wide, use a plain arched window instead).

- **Principle:** no guide entry gives the rose window. T7's window recipe is domestic (panes + shutters + flower box); T35's slits are fortress. The rose window is a third grammar: a tall arched opening whose upper part is a dark tracery circle and whose lower part is glass or a dark panel, framed by pale stone. The new content is the circular tracery window as a facade grammar; the amber glass below it is T7/T15 territory and is not claimed as new.
- **Recipe, variant A (screen, f_004/f_005):** a freestanding 1-thick wall ~6 wide x ~12 tall standing on a flat deck, its top a POINTED ARCH, not a flat cap (crop-rose-window.jpg, f_004, f_005 all show the wall narrowing to a point at the top). Vertical layout, 12 courses total: sill course 0; amber glass ~4 wide x ~5 tall (courses 1-5) crossed by dark mullion bars (1 vertical + 5-7 horizontal); rose circle ~4 diameter (courses 6-9) built from dark tracery in a radial pattern, spokes from a center ring to the rim (8 spokes suggested, builder's choice); course 10 is the pointed-top transition toward apex course 11, 1 block at the center. Pale stone surround ~1 block thick around the whole opening. Warning: the screen has no volume behind it, so from oblique angles it reads as a flat card; site it as an end wall (which is what the reel does), never as a freestanding billboard in open ground. No buttresses at its base: the pilasters visible below are the nave's corner piers under the deck, not part of the rose wall. Identity note: the screen (f_004/f_005) and the east volume's rose-arch front (f_006/f_007) share width and arrangement and may be the same wall at two stages; not proven, so the material count folds the screen into the east volume (see 02) and does not count it twice. If the screen is a separate wall from the east volume front, add ~33-40 pale, ~20 amber, ~50-60 tracery (see the conditional row in 02); the material count folds it into the east volume only because the identity is unproven, not because the cost disappears.
- **Recipe, variant B (in-wall, f_012/f_014):** the rose sits in a real tower wall ~8-10 wide: rose ~4 diameter near the top, dark panel below it ~3 wide x ~6-7 tall (dark grey, gridded in f_013/f_014 and flat in f_012, material unresolved, NOT amber, see A18), pale stone surround ~1. Vertical layout as variant A, but the wall continues as a tower shaft below and the rose is one opening in a solid wall, so there is no false-front warning. Note: f_012 and f_014 show the same rose, surround, and pilasters, but the panel differs (flat in f_012, gridded in f_013/f_014), so the gridded panel is confirmed only in f_013/f_014. Variant B is confirmed as a rose in a tower wall; the panel grid detail rests on f_013/f_014, not on f_012.
- **Variant C (f_011 twin frame): WITHHELD, do not build.** f_011 shows an open dark frame with TWO rose circles side by side, ~2x the width of a single rose wall. No finished twin rose exists in any later frame, and f_012's single rose is on a different volume (or the same tower in another state), so the twin frame cannot be the construction stage of the single rose. Recorded as an unresolved frame state only.
- **Pointed-arch pattern (generic construction, NOT frame-measured):** the reel's exact arch stepping is not resolvable at this resolution, so the pattern below is standard pointed-arch construction, labeled as such, and it has been checked to sum to its stated size. For a 3-wide x 10-tall opening (courses 0-9, exactly 10 courses): jambs straight for courses 0-7 (3 wide); course 8 is the spring line, upside-down stairs at both spring points facing inward, the opening narrowing to 1 wide at the center; course 9 is the apex, 1 block at the center. The arch ring is 1 block proud of the wall (per T15). Text diagram:
  ```
  course 9:       [#]        apex, 1 block
  course 8:     [/] [\]      spring, stairs facing inward
  course 7:     |   |        jambs, 3 wide
  ...
  course 0:     |   |        sill
  ```
  The pointed top occupies the top 2 courses including the spring line for a 3-wide opening (spring course 8 and apex course 9). For wider openings, scale the same rule: step in 1 per course above the last straight jamb course until the two sides meet (a 5-wide opening: spring course 3 wide, then 1 wide, then the apex, 3 courses above the last straight jamb course, 11 courses total).
- **Mini note:** below ~6-wide faces, shrink the rose to ~3 diameter and drop the lower glass to ~2 wide x ~4 tall, or the tracery crowds into noise; below that, use a plain arched window (T7/T15) instead. A rose needs room to be a circle, not a blob.
- **Apply when:** churches, cathedrals, any tall pale volume whose front face needs one dominant window. **NOT when:** the face already carries a belfry, a belvedere, or a stepped gable crown (one crown grammar per face, the rose IS the face's crown); domestic builds (a rose on a cottage reads as a church door, not a window).
- **Prevents:** the "blank gable" read, a tall pale gable with only slit windows reads as a barn; the rose gives the face a focal point that is also a window. (OMGGamer ref: f_004 rose screen going up; f_005 tracery + amber glass from behind; f_012/f_014 finished rose in a tower wall.)
- **Weakness, stated honestly:** at showcase distance (f_014/f_015) the rose shrinks to a dark disc that reads like a wall clock, while the amber arch below it does the actual gothic work; and the recipe only pays off at mega band, on faces ~6 wide and up. For almost every smaller build this candidate cancels itself back to a plain arched window. Its audience is builders of large church/cathedral builds, not the general mini/mid user.

### 4. Cross finial + turret cluster (DEMOTED to T34, WITH AN EXPLICIT REVISION QUESTION)

**Status: demoted-to-sub-bullet (T34), plus a revision question for lock time, not a silent sub-bullet.** **Band: mid** (spire width >7 for the dark stepped taper; turrets apply at all scales).

- **Principle:** T34 already covers the stepped dark spire + accent + finial, including the subordinate spire. What the reel adds is two details T34 does not state: the finial is a CROSS (a thin rod with crossbars, not a plain spike), and the crown is flanked by a CLUSTER of corner turrets (small towers with windows, f_007/f_013/f_014) plus ridge spikes, not a single subordinate spire.
- **Recipe:** main spire as T34 (dark stepped taper, inset ~1 block per 2-3 rise). Finial: thin dark rod ~3-4 tall with a crossbar near the top (iron bars read best at distance, exact block not resolvable); the cross reads at distance where a plain spike vanishes. Turret cluster: corner turrets (small towers ~2-3 wide with small windows) rising from the tower's mid-height to above the roofline, each with its own tiny finial; ridge spikes (1-block finials) spaced along the ridge between them. Pinnacles (mini-spires with no windows, 1-2 wide, ~3-5 tall) sit on parapet and buttress caps where the reel shows them; do not call a turret a pinnacle.
- **The "one cross per build" rule is DELETED.** f_007 shows a cross on the spire tip AND a cross on the ridge to the left; f_013 shows two pinnacles with crosses plus a ridge cross. The reel contradicts the rule in its own frames.
- **Revision question for Luki at lock time (T34 cap conflict):** T34 caps subordinate spires at "at most one" and accent blocks at "exactly ONE". This reel shows a cluster of corner turrets plus multiple crosses. Question: does T34's "at most one subordinate spire" cover turrets (which are towers, not spires), or only spires? And is the cross finial a T34 finial variant (cross instead of spike) or a separate grammar? This is asked, not folded silently: if the cluster is legitimate gothic grammar, T34's cap needs an explicit revision, not a sub-bullet that hides the conflict.
- **Apply when:** church/cathedral towers, any dark spire that needs a readable tip and a broken skyline. **NOT when:** the shaft is already dark (no contrast, T34's guard still holds); pale spires (a cross finial on a pale spire reads as a church steeple, which is a different grammar, use it deliberately, not by accident).
- **Prevents:** the "vanishing tip" (a plain spike on a dark spire disappears against the sky) and the "lonely spire" (one spire on a wide roof reads as an antenna; the turret cluster ties it to the roofline). (OMGGamer ref: f_007 main spire + cross finial + corner turrets, zoom crop `crop-tower-top.jpg`; f_010 ridge spikes; f_014/f_015 finished skyline.)
- **Weakness, stated honestly:** at showcase distance the cross finial is small and nearly lost against the sky (crop-tower-top.jpg), and the spire still reads without it. The turret cluster is the part that carries the skyline, not the cross.

## Cheap substitutes and survival notes

The build as shown costs ~3602-4150 pale stone (minus 140 pale if the flat deck is removed, see 02), ~732-920 dark slate, ~305 wood, ~238-300 amber glass, ~283-390 dark tracery pieces (see 02 for the recomputed table, totals verified to sum from the rows). That is a large survival bill for an empty shell with no interior, no storage, and no bed. Substitutes, all honest: pale stone can be any stone-brick family block, no quartz needed; white trim can be white concrete or calcite instead of quartz (quartz means the Nether, calcite means finding a geode); amber glass needs brown dye (cocoa beans from a jungle), substitute orange stained glass or plain glass if there is no jungle near spawn; dark tracery can be fences (wood) instead of iron bars if iron is short, the read is the same at distance. Tower-top work (~45 up) must be done from a scaffold platform with a railing, or in creative with flight; never free-climb to place a 1-2-wide turret at that height. Mob-proofing is not shown in the reel: the open belfry-like story and the tall glazed windows need interior lighting (T6) or the tower will spawn mobs; the reel gives no lighting plan beyond the three chandeliers.

## Naming note (A20): church/cathedral grammar, not "gothic castle"

The foldable grammar from this reel is church/cathedral grammar: rose window, pointed-arch window. What is genuinely gothic in the frames: rose window, pointed arch, tracery, turret cluster, verticality. What is ABSENT, and most diagnostic for gothic: flying buttress (the piers here are engaged pilasters, no flyer anywhere in f_003-f_005), ribbed vault (no interior), narrow lancets (the windows here are ~3 wide, not lancets), polychrome stained glass (only amber monochrome here), gargoyles. The building's function is a church/cathedral (nave, rose window, chandeliers, graveyard, crosses), not a castle: no keep, no curtain wall, no battlement, no defensive gate. The reel's title says "Gothic Castle", but the guide should not bless "gothic" as a technique family from this reel, and the word "castle" should not be attached to the folded techniques. Candidates are labeled church/cathedral grammar. The graveyard crosses, dead trees, and chandeliers are generic haunted dressing, not gothic markers.
