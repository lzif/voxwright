## 1. Machicolation gallery band — KEEP

**Reason:** Frame-verified, genuinely new. Frame 03 shows the full composition:
a course projecting past the wall face, topped with a crenellated parapet, with a
row of pointed-arch openings hanging below it and a shadow gap underneath.
Frame 06 confirms the same band at the second-stage transition; frame 07 confirms
it repeats at every stage change. This is NOT T7's parapet (a rim only — alternating
high/low merlons on a slab drip lip, no projection, no arch row) and NOT T15's
arch (a construction recipe for ≥2-wide openings, not a gallery composition).
The novelty is the composition: projection + arch row + shadow gap + repetition
at every transition. The "gallery XOR T7 plain parapet" guard is correct and
necessary — the gallery already contains a parapet.
**Stage-3 notes:** cross-reference T15 for the arch construction instead of
re-specifying it; write the arch height as 2–4 tall (frame 03 shows ~3–4, stage 1
said 2–3).

## 2. Stepped tower staging — KEEP

**Reason:** Frame-verified, genuinely new. Frame 07 shows three discrete stages
(wide base → narrower second → narrower third → spire), each transition marked
by a gallery band. T4's compound hierarchy composes *multiple* towers (flankers +
one dominant) — this is one tower narrowing itself. T13 governs ≤6×6 footprints
only. The ≥8×8 base guard is exactly what keeps this from collapsing into T13's
territory, and the "never another full stage on top" + "max 4 stages" guards are
sound. The base-stage-tallest (~40–50%) proportion matches frame 07.
**Stage-3 notes:** fold candidate 5's massing guard in here (see §5).

## 3. Stepped dark spire with accent + finial — KEEP

**Reason:** Frame-verified, genuinely new. Frame 05: tall dark stepped taper
against the pale shaft, exactly one red accent block near the tip, thin finial
spike above it. Frame 07: the subordinate spire on a side turret at ~60% height
with no accent — the accent-belongs-to-main-only rule is frame-true. This is
NOT T4's pyramid roof (pavilion scale, 1:1 steps, slab + finial cap) — the heavy
spire is roughly as tall as the stages below it with a steeper stepped profile —
and NOT T4's generic "one vertical accent" (a principle, not a construction).
The dark-vs-pale contrast requirement is load-bearing: without it the spire
vanishes into the shaft.
**Stage-3 notes:** keep the step ratio (1 per 2–3 rise) flagged as an estimate and
the accent material as "red family" — neither is block-countable at this
resolution, and nothing structural hinges on either.

## 4. Paired slit window grid — KEEP (standalone, NOT a T7 extension)

**Reason:** This resolves stage 1's flagged question in favor of standalone.
Frame 06 shows it unambiguously: pairs of 1-wide × 2-tall dark slits, no glass,
no shutters, no trim, aligned in a strict grid at three heights on one face,
plus pairs between the pilaster ribs on the upper stage. Frame 07 confirms the
grid across the base shaft (3 rows, 2 pairs per row). It inverts EVERY detail of
T7's window recipe (domestic: glass panes + trapdoor shutters + flower box in
vertical columns) — a T7 variant bullet cannot hold a system that is the
photographic negative of T7's system. It also carries an explicit scope override
of T13's quiet-shaft corollary (one slit per face), valid for wide fortress
towers only — that override needs a standalone entry's scope guard, not a bullet
buried in T7. The pairing + the grid ARE the grammar: single slits read as arrow
loops; paired slits in a grid read as a fortress window system.
**Stage-3 notes:** keep the wide-tower-only guard prominent; the T13 override
must be stated, not implied.

## 5. Fortress massing — FIX (fold into candidate 2, do NOT enter standalone)

**Reason:** The observation is real — frame 07 is exactly "plain noisy shaft,
loud galleries, dark spire, nothing else" — but the construction recipe is
literally candidates 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 combined, and the principle restates T10's
landmark quiet-shaft ("shaft stays QUIET so the read lands on base + crown")
and T13's quiet-shaft corollary with a different justification (mass carries the
read, not crown/anchor). A standalone entry would be a combination rule wearing
a technique's clothes, and it would muddy the T10/T13 scope lines.
**What to change:** do not create a technique 26 for this. Instead, add a
"massing guard" inside candidate 2 (staging): on a staged wide tower, the shaft
gets noise + slit grid only — no balconies, awnings, bays, or trim bands — and
the entire ornament budget goes to the transitions (galleries) and the crown.
The genuinely new bit (wide-tower scope + "spend at transitions") survives there,
where it constrains staging instead of competing with T10/T13.

## 6. Pilaster ribs — DROP (confirmed duplicate of T3)

**Reason:** T3 already specifies exactly this: 1-wide vertical strips, spaced
3–4 apart, strips always on corners, window/door bays between them. Frames 04
and 07 confirm the construction matches (including pairs sitting between the
ribs). The only nuance is material: the reel's ribs are the same stone family
as the wall (shadow-line ribs, slightly proud) rather than T3's contrasting
material. That is a material choice inside the same construction, not a new
technique.
**Stage-3 action:** one-line variant note in T3 — same-material ribs allowed,
shallow relief acceptable (not full T2 posts).

## 7. Weathered stone-brick noise ~30% — DROP (confirmed duplicate of T1)

**Reason:** T1's noise rule (~30% secondary mottled variant) plus its Manor NBT
empirical check (247 + 62 + 46 ≈ 30%) covers this exactly. Frames 02/03/06/07
all show the same clean + cracked + mossy stone-brick mix. Nothing new.

## 8. Crenellated parapet at each stage top — DROP (confirmed duplicate of T7 + T5)

**Reason:** T7's parapet entry (alternating high/low, fortified variant for
towers, stone merlons on formal keeps) and T5's base crenellation already cover
this verb at both ends of the building. It survives only as the top component
inside candidate 1's gallery band, not as a standalone move.
