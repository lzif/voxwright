## Stage 1 — Pattern identification: OMGGamer heavy tower (`~/workspace/heavy-tower-reel/frames/`)

**Build observed:** a massive square stone-brick fortress tower built as a timelapse on a grass hill (~3 stepped stages + a very tall dark spire). One material family throughout (stone bricks + cracked/mossy variants, ~30% noise); ornament concentrated at stage transitions — projecting machicolation gallery bands with pointed-arch openings under crenellated parapets; vertical pilaster ribs on the upper stages; paired 1-wide dark slit windows in a strict grid on the main shaft; a tall dark stepped spire with a single red accent block near the tip, a thin finial spike, and one smaller subordinate spire on a side turret. Frame 04 contains a timelapse scaffold pillar in the sky — a construction artifact, not a technique.

### 1. Machicolation gallery band — the projecting defensive course (NEW)
- **Principle:** Technique 7's parapet is just an alternating high/low rim, and technique 5's base crenellation is the same verb at the bottom. Neither gives the *projecting gallery*: a full course that juts past the wall face, topped by a parapet, with a row of pointed-arch openings hanging below it. The projection + the arch row + the shadow gap underneath are what sell "fortified" — a flat parapet on a flush wall reads as trim; the gallery reads as a fighting platform.
- **Recipe:** At each stage transition (and at least once on a tall shaft), run a 1-block-proud course around the full perimeter, top it with a crenellated parapet (alternating merlons), and cut a row of pointed-arch openings directly below the projecting course — arches 2–3 tall, spaced 2–3 apart, dark/inset so they read as murder holes, not windows. The band must project: flush arches under a flush parapet are just a window row (technique 7), not a gallery. Repeat the band at every stage change so the tower's horizontal rhythm IS the gallery rhythm.
- **Apply when:** fortress/keep/castle towers, defensive walls, any build whose theme is "can withstand attack" (the reel's own framing). **NOT when:** domestic builds (a machicolation band on a cottage reads as cosplay); more than one band language per build (gallery XOR technique 7's plain parapet on the same tower — the gallery already contains a parapet); on shafts <5 wide (the projection eats the wall).
- **Prevents:** the "flat parapet" read — a crenellated rim with nothing under it looks pasted on; the gallery gives the parapet a reason to exist. (OMGGamer ref: frame 03 — first gallery band with arch row under the parapet; frame 06 — close-up of the arches + shadow gap; frames 07–08 — the band repeated at the main-shaft transition.)

### 2. Stepped tower staging — narrow in steps, cap each step (NEW)
- **Principle:** Technique 4's compound hierarchy composes *multiple* towers (flankers + one dominant); technique 13's stacking discipline governs ornament on *narrow* (≤6×6) towers. Neither covers a single WIDE tower that narrows in discrete stages: wide base shaft → narrower second stage → narrower third stage → spire, each stage capped with its own gallery/parapet. The staging is the silhouette: without the steps the tower is an extruded box; with them it reads as engineered mass.
- **Recipe:** Build the tower in 3 stages (2 minimum): each stage 1–2 blocks narrower per side than the one below, each stage transition marked by a full-perimeter gallery band (candidate 1) or at minimum a proud cap course + parapet. Stage heights: base stage tallest (~40–50% of shaft height), upper stages progressively shorter. The top stage is the narrowest and carries the crown grammar (spire, belfry, or belvedere) — never another full stage. Keep the total footprint wide (≥8×8 base) — on ≤6×6 footprints this collapses into technique 13's territory and the steps read as stacking, not staging.
- **Apply when:** fortress towers, keeps, lighthouse-style monuments, any single tower meant to read "heavy". **NOT when:** narrow wizard/fantasy towers (use technique 13 + a loggia/belfry crown); when the build needs a flat roof (staging forces a stepped silhouette — pick one); more than 4 stages (reads as a wedding cake).
- **Prevents:** the extruded-box tower — one unbroken shaft from ground to spire, however tall, reads as a stretched cube; the stages are what give it designed mass. (OMGGamer ref: frames 07–08 — three clear stages + spire; frame 15 — the full wide-to-narrow silhouette; frame 05 — the second stage rising off the first gallery.)

### 3. Stepped dark spire with accent + finial — the heavy crown (NEW)
- **Principle:** Technique 4 covers spires only as a generic "one vertical accent" and pyramid roofs at *pavilion* scale (stepped stair roof, slab + finial cap). The heavy tower's spire is a different animal: a tall (roughly as tall as the stages below it) dark-stone stepped taper that contrasts the pale shaft, carrying exactly one accent block near the tip and a thin finial spike — plus one subordinate spire at ~60% height on a side turret. The dark-vs-pale contrast + the single accent are what make it a crown rather than a roof.
- **Recipe:** Spire material: a dark stone family contrasting the shaft (dark deepslate/stone against pale stone brick). Profile: stepped taper — inset 1 block per 2–3 blocks of rise (steeper than a pyramid roof's 1:1, so the spire reads tall, not squat). Near the tip (2–4 blocks below the finial), place exactly ONE accent block in a saturated color (red in the reel) — one block, never a band. Finish with a thin finial: 1×1 dark spike 2–4 tall. Subordinate spire (optional, at most one): same construction at ~60% height on a corner/side turret, no accent block — the accent belongs to the main spire only.
- **Apply when:** fortress/keep towers, dark-fantasy crowns, any tower whose shaft is pale and needs a dark crown to terminate. **NOT when:** the shaft is already dark (no contrast — the spire vanishes into the shaft); pale/light spires (that's technique 4's pyramid roof, not this); more than one accent block (two red blocks read as a mistake, not a detail).
- **Prevents:** the flat-stop failure (technique 13's named enemy) on wide towers — and the "spire as afterthought" (a 3-block pyramid cap on a 30-block tower reads as a hat, not a crown). (OMGGamer ref: frame 09 — main spire + subordinate spire + red accent + finial; frame 11 — close-up of the stepped profile and the accent; frames 15/17 — the dark spire against the pale shaft.)

### 4. Paired slit window grid — fortification windows (NEW; MED confidence)
- **Principle:** Technique 7's window recipe is domestic (glass panes + shutters + flower box, repeated in vertical columns). Technique 13's quiet-shaft corollary allows at most one narrow slit per face on landmark shafts. The heavy tower does neither: its windows are *pairs* of 1-wide × 2-tall dark slits (no glass, no shutters, no trim), repeated in a strict grid — several pairs per face, aligned in rows and columns across the whole shaft. The pairing + the grid are the grammar: single slits read as arrow loops; paired slits in a grid read as a fortress's window system.
- **Recipe:** Windows as pairs: two 1-wide × 2-tall dark openings (air or dark blocks, never glass) spaced 1 block apart, pairs spaced 3–4 blocks apart horizontally and 4–6 vertically, aligned to a strict grid on every face. No sills, no shutters, no lintels — the grid alignment IS the detail. On the upper stages the pairs sit between pilaster ribs (candidate 6's bays); on the main shaft they sit on the flat wall.
- **Apply when:** fortress/keep/castle shafts, defensive walls, any wide tower where windows must read as fortification, not domesticity. **NOT when:** domestic builds (use technique 7's full window recipe); narrow landmark shafts (technique 13's quiet-shaft corollary — one slit per face — still governs there); when the wall already carries heavy ornament (grid + ornament = noise).
- **Prevents:** the "house windows on a castle" failure — glass + shutters on a fortress shaft kill the fortified read instantly. (OMGGamer ref: frames 07–08 — paired slits in a grid on the main shaft; frame 13 — pairs at three heights on one face; frame 06 — pairs below the gallery band.)
- **Confidence note:** MED — this could be argued as a technique-7 extension (a window variant) rather than a standalone technique; the roast stage should decide whether the pairing+grid grammar is distinct enough to stand alone.

### 5. Fortress massing — quiet shaft, loud transitions (NEW; MED confidence — principle, not construction)
- **Principle:** Technique 10's landmark rule says a landmark shaft stays quiet (≤1 slit per face) so the read lands on base + crown; technique 13's quiet-shaft corollary says the same for crowned narrow towers. The heavy tower inverts the *reason* while keeping the *look*: its shaft is quiet not because a crown carries the read, but because the MASS carries it — and the ornament budget is spent entirely at the stage transitions (galleries) and the crown (spire). The principle: on a wide tower, mass + transitions > surface detail.
- **Recipe (principle only):** Shaft: noise (technique 1) + slit grid (candidate 4) only — no balconies, no awnings, no bays, no trim bands. Transitions: spend everything there — gallery bands, cap courses, parapets. Crown: one strong move (candidate 3's spire). If a face starts accumulating detail beyond noise + slits, the massing has broken — strip it back.
- **Apply when:** wide fortress towers, keeps, monumental shafts. **NOT when:** narrow towers (technique 13 governs); domestic builds (technique 7's layering is the point there); towers whose crown is weak (a quiet shaft + a weak crown = a boring box — the transitions and crown must earn the quiet).
- **Prevents:** the over-decorated fortress — a castle shaft with balconies and flower boxes reads as a house in armor. (OMGGamer ref: frames 15/17 — the full tower: plain noisy shaft, loud galleries, dark spire; nothing else.)
- **Confidence note:** MED — this is a compositional principle that overlaps techniques 10/13; the roast stage may fold it into candidate 2 (staging) as a guard instead of a standalone technique.

### 6. Pilaster ribs — same-material vertical strips (DUPLICATE — confirms technique 3)
- **Observation:** the upper stages carry 1-wide vertical strips running floor-to-roof with window bays between them (frames 04, 07, 08). Technique 3's pilaster strips already specify exactly this: 1-wide vertical strips, spaced 3–4 apart, strips on corners, bays between them. The only nuance here is that the reel's ribs are the *same* stone family as the wall (working as shadow-line ribs) rather than technique 3's contrasting material — a same-material variant worth a one-line note in technique 3, not a new technique.
- **Verdict: DUPLICATE.** Do not propose as new.

### 8. Crenellated parapet at each stage top (DUPLICATE — confirms techniques 7 + 5)

- **Verdict: DUPLICATE.** Do not propose as new — except as the *component* inside candidate 1's gallery band, where the parapet is the gallery's top, not a standalone move.
