## Stage 1 — Pattern identification: Niitty90 floating wizard tower (`~/workspace/niitty-reel/frames/`)

**Build observed:** a tall dark-stone wizard tower (~5–6 stories on a ≤6×6 footprint, deepslate/stone-brick) that levitates — purple stained-glass "magic core" blooming beneath its base in stepped descending discs, iron chains dangling off the underside, corner "root" spikes where it tore free; a second smaller floating tower tethered to it by a narrow dark bridge with chain-lanterns; horned/spired open-colonnade crown with chains rising upward off the pinnacle tips. Frame 01 shows the pre-lift state on the hill; frame 06 (night) is motion-blurred — usable only for the purple-glow read, nothing else.

### 1. Magic-core lift plume — the licensed levitation illusion (NEW)
- **Principle:** The guide's anti-pattern bans floating builds outright. This build is the *licensed exception*: floating is allowed only when the lift itself is constructed — a visible "engine" under the base that the eye reads as the cause of levitation. Without the plume, the tower is just a floating box (still anti-pattern); with it, the float is the point.
- **Recipe:** Directly under the base, stack 4–6 horizontal discs of purple stained glass, each disc 1–2 blocks smaller than the one above (densest at the base, tapering to a point ~8–10 blocks below). Between discs leave 1-block air gaps — the gaps make it read as energy, not a glass column. Scatter 4–8 free-floating single glass shards (1×1–2×1) in the air ring around the plume at varied heights. Purple + dark stone is the canonical pairing; one accent color only, never multicolor.
- **Apply when:** floating towers, floating islands, sky constructs where levitation is the theme. **NOT when:** the build can just sit on the ground (a magic core under a grounded tower reads as a basement on fire); when the plume is wider than the base (it reads as a stand, not a lift — keep it ≤ base width); never multicolor glass (reads as confetti, not energy).
- **Prevents:** the "floating box" anti-pattern — unsupported masses with no visual justification; also the glass-column read (solid stacked glass = a stand, not magic). (Niitty90 ref: frame 02 — stepped glass discs, densest at base, air gaps, free shards visible at left/right.)

### 2. Broken-root base — the detachment scar (NEW)
- **Principle:** A levitating tower needs the bottom to tell a story: it *used to be* rooted. Pointed downward stone spikes at the base corners sell the "torn free" moment — without them the base reads as a clean flat floor that simply hovers; with them it reads as a structure ripped out of the ground.
- **Recipe:** At each base corner (and optionally mid-edge), run the corner column DOWN past the base floor as a 2–4-block inverted-pyramid taper (stepping 1 block inward per 1 down, ending in a point). Uneven lengths per corner (±1) — symmetric spikes read as landing gear. Keep the spikes in the wall stone family; no contrasting material. This sits ABOVE the magic plume (recipe 1 hangs beneath/between the spikes).
- **Apply when:** any levitating structure; also works for "lifted chunk" floating islands (grass-topped underside tapering to a rock point). **NOT when:** the structure never had a ground state in its story (a floating island built as always-airborne gets a tapered underside, not broken roots — different verb); never symmetrical/perfect spikes (landing-gear read).
- **Prevents:** the "hovering floor" — a flat base plane floating reads as a misplaced build; the spikes are what convert a bug into a feature. (Niitty90 ref: frame 02 — downward stone spikes at base corners, plume between them; frame 01 pre-lift shows the base tapering into leg-like projections where it still meets the hill.)

### 3. Gravity-defying chain finials (NEW — upward variant of T7's chain vocabulary)
- **Principle:** Technique 7 lists chains as "bits" (hanging dressing); technique 19's hoist grammar hangs chains with loads. This is the inverse move: chains that rise UPWARD off the pinnacle tips into the sky. Gravity-defying chains are the cheapest possible magic signal — they break physics in one block column and the whole crown reads as enchanted.
- **Recipe:** On each spire/pinnacle tip, place a 1-block chain anchor, then run iron chain blocks 3–6 upward with nothing at the end — the dangling-into-nothing read is the point. 1 finial per pinnacle, uniform heights (±1); all pinnacles get one or none get one. Complement with normal downward chains off the base corners at varied lengths (some ending in lanterns) — up AND down chains frame the build's vertical axis as the "magic channel."
- **Apply when:** wizard/fantasy crowns, floating structures, any build where the theme is magic. **NOT when:** realistic builds (a chain going up from a Tudor chimney reads as a build error); more than one vertical chain language per build (up-finials XOR load-bearing hoists — don't mix with technique 19's working chains).
- **Prevents:** a fantasy tower that reads as merely "dark" — the upward chains are what make it *wizard*. (Niitty90 ref: frame 05 — chains rising off every pinnacle tip, clearly visible against sky; frame 02 — downward chains off base corners at varied lengths.)

### 4. Tether bridge between floaters (extends technique 17's bridge anatomy; new sub-case)
- **Principle:** Technique 17's bridge is path-to-path across water, with landings mandatory. A bridge between two *floating* bodies crosses a void instead of water — and the void IS the crossing, so the landings logic inverts: both ends anchor into the structures' mid-stories, and the construction grammar must read as light as possible (a heavy bridge between two floaters reads as one rigid dumbbell, not two levitating bodies).
- **Recipe:** Narrow span (1–2 wide) of dark stone/stone brick, no railings or low 1-high fence stubs only, connecting the two towers at matching story heights — never at the crowns (top-heavy read). Under the span, hang 2–3 chains at varied lengths ending in lanterns (the lit version of technique 3's dangling chains) — they dress the under-deck the way technique 22 dresses pier undersides, but here they also sell the tether's lightness. The bridge must be visibly thinner than both bodies it joins.
- **Apply when:** floating clusters, sky-island archipelagos, any two airborne structures. **NOT when:** grounded builds (use technique 17's full anatomy — deck + rails + landings, path to path); wider than 2 (reads as a platform, not a tether); at crown height (crown-to-crown connections read as a single mass — connect mid-shaft instead).
- **Prevents:** the dumbbell read — two floaters rigidly joined into one object; and the gangplank-to-nowhere (a bridge with one end in empty air). (Niitty90 ref: frames 03–04 — dark narrow span between the main and middle towers at mid-story, chain-lanterns hanging under it, a player crossing sells the scale.)

### 5. Floater constellation — size hierarchy for floating clusters (NEW)
- **Principle:** One floating tower is a marvel; two same-size floating towers read as copy-paste. The cluster grammar: one dominant floater carrying the full illusion kit (plume + chains + crown), one satellite at ~60–70% of its height with a smaller, simpler plume — the size difference is what makes the pair read as a composed cluster instead of a duplicated build.
- **Recipe:** Dominant: full recipe (techniques 1–3). Satellite: same material family, same window grammar, but ~2/3 height, one simple 3-disc plume, fewer or no chains, plain or simple cap (never a competing crown). Connect with the tether bridge (technique 4). Keep the satellite within ~10 blocks of the dominant — too far and the tether reads as a causeway; too close and the plumes merge visually.
- **Apply when:** floating island clusters, wizard complexes, sky cities in miniature. **NOT when:** only one floater exists (nothing to hierarchize); three+ floaters (then it's a sky-archipelago — vary all three sizes and connect with a path network, not one tether).
- **Prevents:** the duplicated-floater read; and the competing-crown failure (two ornate crowns at the same height = two builds fighting). (Niitty90 ref: frame 03 — tall main tower with full plume vs. the shorter middle tower with a smaller purple base; frame 04 — the satellite's simpler construction.)

### 6. Open loggia crown (NEW — distinct from T25's open belfry)
- **Principle:** Technique 25's open belfry shows a program (the bell) in the top 2–3 blocks. This crown is a different grammar: the entire top story is an open-air colonnade — corner pillars with no walls between them, ~7 blocks tall — capped with horned spired pinnacles. It's not a belfry (nothing hangs in it); it's a *viewing loggia*, and the pillars themselves are the silhouette.
- **Recipe:** Top story: 4 corner pillars of the shaft stone, each 1×1 (or 2×2 on wider towers), 6–8 tall, full height with NO wall infill between them — the story is 100% openings. Cap each pillar with a stepped spire (stair-block taper, 3–4 blocks) ending in a technique-3 chain finial. The deck floor between the pillars stays solid (it's a walkable top); light the interior with lanterns so it reads occupied at range (warm glow through the open sides).
- **Apply when:** wizard/fantasy tower tops, lookout crowns, any build whose top should read as occupied air rather than a cap. **NOT when:** the top needs to show a single object (use T25's belfry — one crown grammar per tower); occupiable interior below with a plain roof above (that's T4's belvedere instead); on towers <4 stories (a loggia crown on a short tower reads as a pergola on a stump).
- **Prevents:** the flat-stop failure (T13's named enemy — "a chimney that forgot its fireplace"); and the belfry misread (hanging nothing in an open top reads as unfinished — the loggia's pillars + spires are what say "finished"). (Niitty90 ref: frame 05 — 4 tall corner pillars, fully open sides, stepped spires with upward chains, lit interior visible, player on the deck.)

### 7. Night inversion — the core as the light source (NEW; LOW-CONFIDENCE — needs the full build to verify)
- **Principle:** By day the dark tower dominates and the plume is an accent; at night the build inverts — the purple core becomes the primary light source and the tower becomes a silhouette hanging from its own glow. Designing the underside as the night anchor is what makes a floating build work in both lighting conditions instead of only at noon.
- **Recipe (principle only — construction unverified):** place the light source INSIDE or directly behind the glass plume so the glass reads as glowing (not a lit block used as wall material — T6's guard still applies; the light is the plume's *contents*, never its skin). Keep the tower's own windows lit warm (amber) so the read is purple-core + warm-windows against black stone.
- **Apply when:** any floating build the player will see at night. **NOT when:** the plume is meant to be subtle (then light the windows only and let the core stay dark glass — one night-lighting story per build, never a purple core AND a full lantern grid competing).
- **Prevents:** the night-vanish — a dark floating tower at night with no underside light reads as a black rectangle that the plume can't save.
- **Inference flag:** frame 06 is motion-blurred — it shows only dark tower silhouettes + a purple glow patch at core height. I cannot verify the light-block construction. This is a *principle* claim (design for the inversion), not a *recipe* claim — the roast stage should downgrade it to a guard inside technique 1 unless the full YouTube build verifies the construction. (Niitty90 ref: frame 06 — purple glow at the plume position only; ideas.md's "night shots where the purple core glows" corroborates the read.)
