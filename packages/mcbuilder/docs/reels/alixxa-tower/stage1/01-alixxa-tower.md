## Build observed

A cozy medieval tower on a flower meadow, in a village context — other builds (a teal-roofed house, a cottage) and big custom trees stand in the background in every frame. One volume at a time, bottom-up, each stage dressed before the next starts. Arc across the 17 frames: f_001 bare dirt mound on a stone-slab patio; f_002 overlay "7 ↔ 7"; f_004 hollow 7×7 stone-brick ring ~2–3 high; f_005 spruce plank floor, corner pillars, ground flower bed, lantern posts, entrance stair flight; f_006 walls rising, ladder on the inner back wall; f_007 shaft complete with slit windows and a flat spruce-plank top deck with a 1×1 hole (hatch); f_008 overlay "15"; f_009 ground-floor dressing (door, trapdoor awning, shuttered window); f_010 upper-level dressing (trapdoor awnings + flower boxes + corner lanterns); f_011 trapdoor railing around the flat top rim; f_012–f_013 narrower upper shaft rising from the deck center, terrace doorway on the front face at the ring level; f_014 overlay "18"; f_015 wide flat spruce-slab skirt roof over the terrace ring; f_016 crenellated rim on the upper shaft + upper-shaft awnings and lanterns; f_017 steep stepped spruce pyramid crown + thin spire rod. Final frame is the finished tower.

Palette: pale stone brick, warm spruce (planks / slabs / stairs / trapdoors), red flowers + trailing vines in the boxes, lanterns. Two materials only on the tower itself — the whole read is pale-vs-warm.

## Terminology (one term per element)

- **Skirt roof:** the wide flat spruce-slab roof at the stage junction, overhanging the lower shaft ~2 blocks per side, with the upper shaft poking through its center. NOT a floor, NOT a roof cap — it is a mid-tower eave.
- **Terrace ring:** the ~1-block-wide walkable ring around the upper shaft at the top of the lower shaft, railed with spruce trapdoors, roofed by the skirt roof. NOT a projecting balcony — its floor is the lower shaft's top, not a cantilevered deck.
- **Triplet slit windows:** three 1-wide × 3-tall unglazed slits in a row per face, each with a trapdoor awning above and (upper level) a flower box below.
- **Upper stage / second stage:** the narrower (~5×5) shaft rising from the terrace ring, 18 tall per the reel's label.
- **Stepped pyramid crown:** the steep spruce pyramid on the upper shaft, stepped 1-in-1, slab eave overhanging ~1, thin rod spire at the peak.

## Candidates

### 1. Mid-tower skirt roof as stage separator — PROPOSED NEW (was: nothing)

**Status: proposed NEW (stage 1, unroasted).** **Band: mini** (7×7 base, ~5×5 upper).

- **Principle:** T33 covers stepped tower staging at mega scale (≥8×8 base, 3 stages, gallery bands at the transitions) and its mini substitution says "never narrow a tower in discrete 1-block stages — taper instead". This tower narrows in ONE discrete step (7×7 → ~5×5) at mini scale and does not read as a wedding cake — because the setback is not a tier, it is a ROOF. The flat slab skirt at the junction turns the stage change into an eave: the eye reads "lower tower with a roof, upper tower growing through it", not "box on a box". The skirt is what rescues a two-stage mini tower from the wedding-cake read.
- **Recipe:** Lower shaft 7×7, 15 tall. At its top, the upper shaft (~5×5, 18 tall) rises from the center, leaving a ~1-block terrace ring around it. Over the ring, lay a flat spruce-slab roof ~11×11 — overhang ~2 blocks per side beyond the lower shaft, ~3 beyond the upper shaft — with the upper shaft poking through its center. The skirt's edge is a plain slab rim (no fascia course visible); its underside is exposed slabs. Railing: spruce trapdoors around the ring's outer edge, installed BEFORE the skirt (f_011) — the railing is on the ring, the skirt roofs over it.
- **The terrace doorway:** the upper shaft carries a dark doorway opening (~2 wide × ~3 tall) on the front face at the ring level (f_012, persists to f_017) — the ring is reachable from inside the upper shaft, so it is a real walkable terrace, not a ledge. No door is installed; the interior is not visible.
- **Apply when:** any two-stage tower at mini scale (≤9×9 base) where the stages need a legible break. **NOT when:** the stage change is already marked by a gallery band (T32 — one transition grammar per tower); on single-stage towers (there is nothing to separate); when the upper stage is ≤3×3 (the ring becomes a gutter, not a terrace — no room to stand).
- **Prevents:** the "box on a box" read — a narrower shaft sitting directly on a wider shaft with no transition reads as a stacking mistake; the skirt makes the joint a roof. (Alixxa ref: f_011 rim railing; f_012–f_014 upper shaft rising; f_015 skirt laid; f_017 finished two-stage tower.)
- **Lint reasoning (for the roast):** the wedding-cake lint flags 3+ consecutive box-like setbacks on the main mass. This build has ONE setback (7→5) plus a pyramid crown (pointy pyramids, apex ≤3, are exempt). No flag is expected — but the reasoning depends on the skirt doing the work, so a fold must keep the skirt mandatory, not optional.

### 2. Triplet slit windows with trapdoor awning + flower box — PROPOSED T7 SUB-ENTRY (was: nothing)

**Status: proposed T7 sub-entry (stage 1, unroasted).** **Band: mini** (face 7 wide).

- **Principle:** T7's window recipe is domestic and glazed (2-high glass panes + trapdoor shutters + greenery box + hanging lantern). T35's slit grammar is fortress and undressed (paired 1×2 dark slits in a strict grid, no sills, no shutters, no trim). This build does neither: its windows are TRIPLETS of 1×3 unglazed slits, dressed domestically — a trapdoor awning above each slit and a flower box below (upper level). The dressing is the technique: the same slit that would read as an arrow loop reads as a cozy cottage window once it has an awning and a flower box. This is the "cozy" in "cozy medieval tower" — fortress openings, domestic dressing.
- **Recipe:** Per face, per story: three 1-wide × 3-tall unglazed slits in a row, spaced 1 block apart (group spans ~5 of the 7-wide face, centered). Above each slit: one open spruce trapdoor as an awning, top-hinged, jutting ~1 block. Below the upper-level slits: one flower box — spruce trapdoor planter with flowers + trailing vines — spanning the triplet (~5 wide). Lower level (ground story): 2 slits on the left face, 1×2 tall, with trapdoor shutters (closed, flat across the opening) instead of awnings. No glass anywhere; the slits read dark (interior unlit / backed by darkness — no sky is visible through them in any frame). Repeat the triplet on every story and every face that needs windows; the ground story uses the shorter shuttered variant.
- **Novelty (vs T7 / T35):** T7's greenery box hangs under GLAZED panes; T35's slits are undressed by definition. Neither gives an unglazed slit WITH dressing. The awning + box system is a structured frame around the slit group — which matters for the lint question below.
- **Apply when:** small towers and keeps that need to read fortified AND lived-in at the same time. **NOT when:** pure fortress builds (use T35's undressed grid — flower boxes on a siege tower read as cosplay); pure domestic builds (use T7's glazed recipe — unglazed slits read as arrow loops, not windows); when the face already carries a balcony or bay on the same story (one ornament per story per face — T13's rule is scoped to ≤6×6, but the spirit holds here).
- **Prevents:** the "arrow-loop house" read — undressed slits make a small tower read as a bunker; the "glass cottage" read — glazed panes make the same tower read as a house with delusions. The dressed slit sits between them. (Alixxa ref: f_007 slits first visible; f_009 ground-floor shuttered window + door awning; f_010 upper awnings + flower boxes; f_013 upper-shaft slits; f_016 upper-shaft awnings.)
- **LINT QUESTION — OPEN, do not resolve in stage 1:** the pareidolia lint flags twin symmetric 1-wide slits on any face ≤10 wide. This build has TRIPLETS on 7-wide faces. A triplet is not a twin pair, but it is symmetric — and the lint's exemption is for "trimmed pairs (structured frame around both slits)". The awning + flower-box system is arguably a structured frame around the whole triplet, which would exempt it — but whether the lint fires on dressed triplets is a lint-rule question for the roast/review, not a frame-reading question. Record it, do not claim the source is lint-clean.

### 3. Ruined-mound terrain base — PROPOSED T5 SUB-ENTRY (was: nothing)

**Status: proposed T5 sub-entry (stage 1, unroasted).** **Band: mini** (site ~15×15).

- **Principle:** T5 says "sit it in the terrain" (patchy paths, plants around the base — not a clean cutout on flat grass) and gives the stepped civic plinth (2 clean courses, each 1 wider per side). This build does neither: the tower sits on an irregular dirt MOUND ~2 high — a patchwork of dirt / coarse dirt / mossy cobble / stone, no clean courses, ragged edge — ringed by a stone-brick slab patio ~15×15 that bleeds into the flower meadow. The mound is the first thing built (f_001) and it does real work: the tower reads as grown out of a ruin mound rather than placed on grass, and the patio gives the irregular mound a hard edge to sit in.
- **Recipe:** Before any wall goes up, shape a low irregular mound ~2 high under the footprint: mixed dirt / coarse dirt / mossy cobble / stone in irregular patches (no single material dominant, no straight edges). Around it, lay a stone-brick slab patio ~15×15 (roughly 2× the footprint per side), its edge left ragged — slabs stepping out 1 block at irregular intervals, grass and flowers growing up to and into the edge. The tower's walls sit directly on the mound, not on the patio.
- **Apply when:** standalone towers and keeps on flat meadow/grass where a clean plinth would read as a parking lot. **NOT when:** the build already has a formal plinth or terrace (one ground treatment per build); on slopes (use T5's walk-the-foundation-down-the-hill instead); on builds meant to read as newly built (a ruin mound is age — it fights a new-build story).
- **Prevents:** the "placed on grass" read — a tower dropped on flat turf with a clean dirt square under it reads as spawned, not built. (Alixxa ref: f_001 mound + patio before any wall; f_017 finished tower still sitting in the same mound.)
- **Caveat:** whether the mound is built terrain work or dressed natural terrain is UNRESOLVABLE from the frames — it is present from f_001 with no shaping visible. The recipe above is "shape a mound", which is the follower instruction either way.

### 4. Steep warm stepped pyramid crown with spire finial — PROPOSED NEW, PROVISIONAL (was: nothing)

**Status: proposed NEW, PROVISIONAL — smoke test required before folding (stage 1, unroasted).** **Band: mini** (shaft ~5×5).

- **Principle:** T4's pyramid entry is pavilion-scale (gazebo: stepped stair roof, squat, slab + finial cap). T34's spire is a tall dark-stone stepped taper with exactly one accent block and a finial — dark vs pale contrast is the whole point. This crown is neither: a STEEP warm spruce pyramid on a tower shaft — stepped 1-in-1 courses from a ~7×7 eave to an apex ≤2, ~7 tall on a ~5×5 shaft (rise ≈ 1.4× the shaft width — far steeper than a pavilion pyramid), capped with a thin rod spire ~3 tall. Warm-on-pale, not dark-on-pale: the crown reads by silhouette and warmth, not by contrast. It is the tower's third material moment (stone shaft → slab skirt → spruce pyramid) and the steepness is what makes it a crown rather than a hat.
- **Recipe (PROVISIONAL — step counts are frame estimates, not a tested build):** On the upper shaft's top rim, lay a slab eave course overhanging ~1 block on all sides (~7×7). Above it, step spruce stair courses inward 1 block per 1 block of rise (~7 courses: 7 → 5 → 3 → 1, then a cap ≤2 wide) — stair blocks facing outward, soffits exposed underneath. Cap with a slab + thin rod spire ~3 tall (fence stack or lightning rod — the block is NOT resolvable at this resolution; do not assert one). Keep the pyramid steep: rise ≥ shaft width, or it reads as a pavilion roof dropped on a tower.
- **Apply when:** small towers (shaft ≤7×7) that need a finished top and already carry a warm accent somewhere (skirt roof, awnings, door) for the pyramid to echo. **NOT when:** the shaft is already dark (no read — a dark pyramid on a dark shaft vanishes; use T34's contrast logic instead); the tower already carries a belvedere, belfry, or gable crown (one crown grammar per tower); wide shafts (≥8×8 — the pyramid becomes a roof, use T4's roof grammar).
- **Prevents:** the "flat-topped tower" read (T13's named enemy) at mini scale — and the "spire as afterthought" (a 3-block cap on a 30-block tower reads as a hat; this pyramid is ~7 tall on an 18-block upper stage, ~1/4 of the stage height, so it holds its own).
- **SMOKE TEST REQUIRED — do not fold on frames alone:** the C2 stepped-gable crown is BLOCKED (tower roof geometry unresolved) and this is a different geometry (pyramid, not gable), so the C2 block does not apply — but the same caution does. What a smoke build must settle: the exact course count and setback rhythm (1-in-1 vs 1-in-2 on the upper courses), the eave overhang (slab vs stair edge), the apex construction (cap ≤2 + spire base), and whether the f_016 crenellated rim survives under the pyramid eave or must be removed (see the open question below). (Alixxa ref: f_016 rim before the crown; f_017 pyramid + spire, final frame.)
- **Cross-ref:** T34's "one accent block" rule does NOT apply here — there is no accent block on this pyramid, and none is needed: the warm spruce against pale stone is the accent. Do not add one.
