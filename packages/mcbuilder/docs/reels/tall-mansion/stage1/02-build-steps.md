## Method and camera caveat

- Each step below names what is NEW vs the previous frame. Tags: **VISIBLE** = directly seen in the frames; **INFERRED** = order deduced (e.g. walls must precede the roof frame that sits on them, even though both appear in the same 1-second interval).
- **The camera orbits.** f_001 is the only rear/high view; f_002 swings ~180° to the front-left, and f_003–f_012 stay mostly frontal with a slow drift. An element "new" in f_002 that sits on a face hidden in f_001 may have been built earlier — where that applies, it is flagged. The one safe check: f_001 shows the interior as open grass from above, so the floor and staircase genuinely were not there at f_001.
- **The video starts mid-build.** At f_001 the ground-floor walls are already up on three sides. The footprint → walls transition is NOT observable; only its traces (the ground outline) survive.
- **1-second granularity limit:** at 1 fps, "appears in the same interval" proves nothing about intent or sub-order inside that second. Two inferences from the first draft are demoted on this ground (see "Corrections applied"): the dormer hole being "planned into the roof laying" and the frame being "applied after the walls" are both UNRESOLVABLE at 1 fps — laying-then-cutting within one second, or columns rising with the walls, are both indistinguishable from the frames.
- No interior shots after f_002, no dusk showcase. Interior furnishing and lighting steps are out of scope — say so plainly below, not guessed.

## Mode assumption

The sequence is written as if built in SURVIVAL (access matters): the wing is a work platform, the stairs go in early so the builder can walk up instead of pillaring on dirt, and every step above floor 1 carries an access note (scaffold / dirt pillar / temp stairs). In creative with flight the same order still works, but the access notes are unnecessary. The locked process's "interior only after shell closed" and "signature first" rules only make sense with flight — this reel's order assumes survival access, and the process amendment (see Verdict) is written from that side.

## Verdict: does the observed order follow the locked staged process?

**No at the macro level — and the finding is about the locked process, not about Life Laps.**

The locked process (2026-11-01) is: footprint → signature element alone → skeleton → shell → detail exterior → interior (only after shell closed) → ruin → landscape last. It was generalized from ONE reel (TrixyBlox Ruined Temple — a single-volume build whose entire identity IS the signature). This reel is the second data point on macro-order, and it contradicts the locked macro-order at every joint. A process that fails its second test is not a yardstick — it is an unproven hypothesis. The deviations below are therefore restated as findings about the process's scope, not as defects in the build.

What the reel actually does: footprint → walls → floor/stairs → front wall + deck → **porch** → **tower** → wing upper deck → wing roof → **tower crown** → accent shaft. The build alternates between volumes instead of finishing one stage cleanly before the next, and the signature is neither first nor finished in one pass.

Findings, explicitly:

- **D1 — Wing-first is the EFFICIENT order (not a defect).** The ordinary wing is shelled first (Steps 1–4, f_001–f_004) and the tower — the "Tall" in the reel's title — only starts rising at Step 5 (f_005). Signature-first on a ~20-block tower means working at height with no adjacent structure (scaffolding from the ground in survival) and blind proportion choices (tower height chosen before the wing exists to judge it against). Wing-first gives the builder a standing platform (the Step 3 deck at ~5 blocks) and a visible proportion reference. "Signature alone first" also conflates a FILMING choice with a BUILDING choice: timelapse creators build the hero piece first because viewers decide in 3 seconds whether to keep watching. Life Laps builds for the finished image, not the hook — and his order is the structurally sane one.
- **D2 — Exterior detail interleaved with shell (classification, not deviation).** The porch (Step 4, f_003→f_004) is built before the tower starts and before any roof exists. If the porch is classified as wing shell rather than "detail exterior", the deviation evaporates — a classification choice, not an observation.
- **D3 — Landscape/grounding interleaved, not last.** Hedge blocks are placed at column bases as each column goes up, starting at Step 1 (f_001). The hedges read as column dressing as much as landscape, but they are landscape material placed early.
- **D4 — Volumes alternate; the signature is crowned last.** The tower is left flat-topped while the wing's upper story and roof are built (Steps 8–10), and only then crowned (Steps 11–12), with the accent shaft dead last. The crown's proportions depend on the finished wing roofline — crowning last is deliberate sequencing, not incompleteness. The locked process implies finishing the signature before moving on; here the signature's finish is one of the last things in the build.
- **D5 — Floor + stairs early is ACCESS, not interior.** The floor and staircase (Step 2) go in while the front wall is still open. In survival the staircase goes in early because you USE it to reach the upper floors; the locked rule would have you pillar up on dirt. This is access, stated as a rule (see Step 2), not a deviation and not ambiguous.

What DOES follow the locked process: footprint first (Step 0, as far as the video shows); the shell-before-roof discipline inside each volume (wing: walls → deck → upper walls → roof frame → slate fill; tower: walls → frame → crown); and the tower itself is built bottom-up in stages (second story → 3 stories → 4th story alongside the wing roof → frame → crown), which matches the guide's stepped tower staging (T33) internally. So the discipline is real, but it lives inside each volume — the macro-order across volumes is the builder's own, not the locked one.
