## Process note — REPLACED (roast A8)

The original note claimed this timelapse "demonstrates the locked staged process". That is factually wrong and has been deleted. The build does NOT follow the locked process at the macro level: the wing is shelled before the tower starts (D1), the porch is built during the shell (D2), hedges go in as columns rise (D3), the tower is crowned last (D4). What it does follow is the discipline INSIDE each volume (walls → deck → upper walls → roof frame → slate; tower bottom-up in stages). The proposed amendment — for Luki to lock or reject, NOT a silent fix — is: **stage discipline applies per volume; signature-first applies when the build's identity is a single volume.** See `02-build-steps.md` (Verdict) for the full amendment. The locked process itself is unchanged anywhere in these files.

## Terminology (one term per element)

- **Beam course:** a 1-block-tall horizontal dark-oak log course at a floor line on the front (hero) face. T2's rule is full-perimeter; this reel applies it to the hero face only.
- **Inter-floor deck:** the flat terracotta slab that is a story's floor / the ceiling below. NOT a roof, NOT a "flat roof plane". (Was called "flat terracotta deck", "inter-floor slab", and "flat terracotta plane" in the drafts — now one term.)
- **Roof-accent shaft:** the 2×2 stone shaft on the wing roof with a barred opening, terracotta cap, dark stepped pyramid, and finial rod. NOT "chimney" — no fireplace exists anywhere, and chimneys do not have barred windows.
- **Tall shaft opening:** the 1-wide × ~4–5-tall dark opening on the tower's front face, mid-tower, with a lantern SITTING on its sill. NOT the loggia.
- **East-face slot:** the ~2-wide × ~4-tall unglazed opening in the tower's plain stone east face, terracotta lintel above, terracotta deck below, open straight through to the sky. NOT a loggia, NOT a room.

### 1. Full-height exposed timber frame on a tall tower — DEMOTED to T2 application note (was: NEW standalone)

**Status: demoted-to-sub-bullet.** Roast: unanimous PIVOT (0 KEEP / 6 PIVOT). **Band: mini** (face ~8 wide; applies ~7–11 wide — below that, T2 corners-only).

- **Principle (rewritten):** T2 already covers this grammar — plates at floor lines, joints as free detail, "an exposed frame IS decoration". What the reel adds is the INVERSION: the frame is applied AFTER the walls are up (Step 7, f_006→f_007), as cladding on a finished stone wall — which contradicts T2's core rule "Pillars and beams define the silhouette; walls fill between them — never the other way around". This entry is therefore a T2 application note, not a new technique: **frame-as-cladding, applied after walls, on the hero face only.**
- **Recipe:** Front (hero) face only — the other faces are plain stone, with corner columns only. 3 vertical dark-oak log columns, 1 block wide, full tower height, bays ~2–3 wide (face ~8 wide). At each floor line a beam course: 1-block-tall horizontal dark-oak log course spanning the front face. At each column/beam crossing a joint block: 1-block protruding joint, log end or chiseled log per T2's joint convention — the reel's joints read as small stepped blocks; stairs vs log ends is NOT resolvable at this resolution, and trapdoors are excluded (they read as shutters, not joints). Infill: stone brick. Frame proud of the infill by **1 block, full stop** — 0 (flush) is the failure mode this recipe itself names ("flush reads as paint"), so the 0–1 range in the draft is deleted. One timber species only.
- **Survival access:** place the frame from a dirt-pillar / scaffold tower at each floor line; aim sideways logs by placing against the face you want them to run along (Java: the log orients along the face normal; on touch, aim at the side face, not the top). The wing deck is the work platform for the lower courses — build the wing first (see the process amendment).
- **Apply when:** tall narrow towers, stair towers, gatehouse keeps — any vertical volume ≥4 stories whose hero face would otherwise be a flat slab. **NOT when:** faces ~7–11 wide is the band — below ~7 use T2 corners-only; on ≥15-wide faces the frame rhythm reads as scaffolding, use pilasters (T3) instead; pale formal builds (the French mansard grammar wants quiet walls — timber frame is a fantasy/Tudor grammar).
- **Prevents:** the "blank stone slab" tower — a tall plain-brick tower with only slit windows reads as a chimney, not a mansion tower. (Life Laps ref: f_007 — columns + floor beams + joint blocks mid-tower; f_012 — full-height frame on the finished tower.)
- **KILL case (none — unanimous PIVOT, no KILL vote):** the PIVOT case is the premise (T2 already covers it) plus the unpinned numbers; both are answered above.

### 6. Terracotta trim system + accent slope — DEMOTED to T1/T4 sub-bullet, recipe now written (was: "Two-material roof contrast", NEW, MED-CONFIDENCE)

**Status: demoted-to-sub-bullet (T1/T4 sub-bullet).** Roast: PIVOT (2 KEEP / 4 PIVOT, no KILL). **Band: mini** (trim rule; applies at all scales).

- **Principle (reframed per C1):** the terracotta plane in f_004 is the INTER-FLOOR DECK, not a finished flat roof — by Step 8 it is built over and by Step 10 it sits under the gable roof. The real two-material story is terracotta as TRIM against dark-slate slopes, plus ONE full terracotta slope on the tower. The "~1/3 of roof area" rule is deleted — it belonged to the flat-plane reading and is meaningless for edge trim.
- **Recipe — trim system:**
  - **Bargeboard stair course:** along each gable edge of the wing roof, run a terracotta stair course — stair blocks facing outward, stepping down from the ridge to the eave (1 block in per 1 block down), following the gable edge. At the ridge the two courses meet; cap the meeting with a terracotta stair or slab (exact meeting block unresolvable at this resolution). The bargeboard is what makes the gable edge read as a designed line instead of a cut slate edge. (Ref: f_009 — terracotta stair edges on the roof frame; f_010 — trim remaining visible after the slate fill.)
  - **Eave fascia:** along the eave, run one terracotta course (blocks or slabs) directly above the wall plate, under the lowest slate course. The fascia is the horizontal line that ties the bargeboard's two ends together. (Ref: f_010 — terracotta fascia course at the eave.)
  - **Echo rule (the only standalone-worthy content):** the same warm accent appears in ≥3 places — roof edges (bargeboard + fascia), crown edges, porch canopy — and that repetition is what ties a multi-volume build together. One accent material, used as trim everywhere, never as a field. If the accent appears only once, it reads as a mistake; if it appears as a field (a whole slope or wall), it stops being trim.
- **Recipe — accent slope (split from the trim system):** the tower's west face is a FULL terracotta stair slope, not trim: pitch ~45° (1:1 stair slope), run ~8 blocks, width = tower depth (~8), from the tower top down to the second-floor balcony level. Build it in terracotta stair blocks, courses stepping down 1-in-1-down. Whether this slope is a roof slope or a separate sloped volume is UNRESOLVED (see candidate 2's roof-geometry block) — its base sits at balcony level, not at an eave.
- **Apply when:** builds with mixed roof forms (steep wing + tower) where one warm accent can tie the volumes together. **NOT when:** single-form roofs (one material is cleaner); formal pale builds (the French grammar wants the roof uniformly dark).
- **Prevents:** the "unfinished flat roof" read is retired with the flat-plane reading. What the trim system prevents: the "cut edge" gable — a dark slate slope whose gable edge is bare slate reads as a sliced roof, not a finished one; and the "one-orphan-accent" read — a single terracotta element with no echo anywhere reads as a material shortage. (Life Laps ref: f_004 — terracotta deck (inter-floor, not roof); f_006 — full terracotta slope on the tower's west face; f_009/f_010 — bargeboard + fascia on the wing roof; f_012 — terracotta crown edges + porch canopy echoing it.)
- **Fold as:** a T1 sub-bullet (two-tone edges) or T4 sub-bullet (two-tone the roof) carrying the accent-echo rule, plus the trim recipe. NOT a standalone numbered entry.
