## Stage 1 — Pattern identification: custom tree (`~/workspace/disruptive-reel/frames/10-tree.jpg`)

**Build observed:** large custom deciduous tree on a build-platform field — thick dark-log trunk (~8–10 blocks of clear shaft) with a pronounced flared root base, branches disappearing into a big rounded leaf canopy (~12–14 wide, visibly two-tone green with small sky-holes), sitting inside a low stone-stair curb ring with leaf bushes and flowers, on a gravel pad. Real Minecraft footage (not a render) — block reads trustworthy; proportion numbers below are my frame measurements, flagged ~approximate.

### 1. Flared root base *(extends technique 5: ground connection)*
- **Principle:** A trunk that meets the ground at full shaft width reads as a planted pole. Real trees flare — splaying root runs outward at the base sells weight and age for almost no extra cost.
- **Recipe:** At ground level, extend the trunk's bottom 2–3 courses outward as descending runs: from each face (and diagonally from the corners), run logs or log-stairs outward 2–4 blocks, dropping 1 block per step. Root reach ≈ 1/3 of the planned canopy radius. Use full logs for chunky roots, stairs for a finer taper. Roots must land ON the terrain, never hover.
- **Apply when:** any custom tree with a trunk ≥2 wide; also large organic pillars. **NOT when:** 1-wide saplings or small decorative trees — roots need mass to read, on a thin trunk they read as clutter.
- **Prevents:** the "telephone pole planted in grass" read. (Custom tree ref: thick root runs splaying visibly wider than the shaft.)

### 2. Tapered trunk with subtle drift *(new)*
- **Principle:** A constant-cross-section trunk reads as a column. Stepping the shaft inward as it rises — plus one small horizontal offset — reads as grown, not built.
- **Recipe:** Start 3×3 (large tree) or 2×2 (medium) at the base; every 4–6 blocks of height, drop the outer ring on one or two faces alternately until 2×2 (or 1×1 for medium) at the crown. Shift the shaft center by 1 block once over the full height for a subtle lean — more than 1 reads as falling. Texture the bark: vertical logs for the shaft, horizontal bark-face logs on outer faces for grain; stripped logs as highlights capped at ~10% or they read as damage.
- **Apply when:** custom trees of any size; also works for giant vines or tentacles. **NOT when:** the trunk must read as architecture (lamp posts, structural pillars — keep those straight).
- **Prevents:** column-read; large flat bark faces with no grain. (Custom tree ref: dark trunk visibly narrowing toward the canopy with strong vertical bark striping.)

### 3. Branch tips carry the canopy — the cluster system *(new; this is the "very easy" method made explicit)*
- **Principle:** The beginner mistake is building the leaf ball first and sticking a trunk under it (lollipop). The easy professional method inverts it: place branches first, then hang exactly one leaf blob on each branch tip — the canopy becomes the union of blobs, automatically irregular, no sculpting skill required.
- **Recipe:** From the top third of the trunk, run 5–8 branches: log segments 2–4 blocks long, angled up-and-out (rise ~1 per 1–2 out), in all compass directions at staggered heights — never two branches at the same height on opposite sides (that reads as stamped). At each branch tip, build one leaf blob 3–5 in diameter (rough sphere: 3×3×3 with corners knocked off, or a flattened 5-wide ellipsoid). Let adjacent blobs overlap by 1 block OR leave 1-block gaps — both read fine; uniform spacing reads as stamped. Cap with one extra blob centered on the trunk top to close the crown. Small sky-holes through the canopy are fine, even desirable — they prove depth.
- **Apply when:** any broadleaf custom tree; single-blob version works for bushes and topiary. **NOT when:** conifers/pines — those want stacked cone layers, a different system (one technique per archetype).
- **Prevents:** lollipop trees (ball on a stick); perfect-sphere canopies that read as green balloons. (Custom tree ref: full rounded canopy with visible sub-blob structure and small see-through gaps.)

### 4. Two-tone canopy for depth *(extends technique 1: material variation)*
- **Principle:** One leaf type over a big canopy flattens into a single green mass. Darker interior + lighter exterior fakes translucency and depth — the same 30%-noise idea as technique 1, zoned instead of scattered.
- **Recipe:** Build each blob's core (inner ~60%) from the darker leaf (dark oak or spruce leaves), then skin the outer 1-block shell with the lighter leaf (oak or birch leaves). On blobs too small for zoning (<5 wide), noise ~30% darker leaves throughout instead. Never invert (light inside, dark outside) — it reads as mold.
- **Apply when:** canopies wider than ~7 blocks (smaller blobs have no room for two zones). **NOT when:** the build's palette mandates one leaf type (stylized/monochrome themes).
- **Prevents:** flat green-blob read; canopy looking like a solid object. (Custom tree ref: darker green depths visible inside, lighter sunlit outer leaves.)

### 5. Canopy proportion rule *(new)*
- **Principle:** Wrong proportions are the #1 silent "something's off" in custom trees, and they're measurable — no taste required. (Frame-measured from the reference, ~approximate.)
- **Recipe:** Let H = trunk height, ground to crown. Canopy total width ≈ H (±20%). Lowest branch junction at ≥ H/2 — branches starting near the ground read as bush, not tree. Trunk base width ≈ H/8 (16-tall tree → 2-wide trunk; 24-tall → 3-wide). Canopy wider than ~1.2H reads as mushroom; narrower than ~0.7H reads as lollipop.
- **Apply when:** planning any custom tree, before placing a single block — it's a measuring step, not a building step. **NOT when:** deliberately stylized trees (bonsai, fantasy) — break it on purpose, never by accident.
- **Prevents:** mushroom/lollipop proportion failures. (Custom tree ref: ~10–12 trunk, ~12–14 canopy — the 1:1 read holds.)

### 6. Finished base: curb ring + underplanting *(extends technique 5: ground connection)*
- **Principle:** A tree on bare grass reads as placed, not planted. A low curb ring plus underplanting turns it into a designed feature — the grounds equivalent of technique 5's foundation course.
- **Recipe:** Ring the root zone (radius = root reach + 1) with a 1-high curb: stone-brick stairs laid as an outer lip, or a single course of stone bricks/walls. Inside the ring: grass/dirt, 2–4 leaf-block bushes (1–2 high single blobs — technique 3's small version), short grass tufts, 3–5 small flowers. Break the ring or step it where a path meets it — a fully closed ring with no entry reads as an abandoned planter.
- **Apply when:** specimen/ornamental trees, town-center trees, garden features. **NOT when:** wild forest trees — those should look unplanted (skip the ring, use technique 8's natural siting instead).
- **Prevents:** "tree dropped on grass" read; the trunk-base dirt seam showing. (Custom tree ref: stone-stair curb ring with leaf bushes and flowers inside, on a gravel pad.)
