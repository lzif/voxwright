### 1. Railing recipe: stone posts + iron bars + slab cap *(extends technique 7's parapet bullet)*

- **Principle:** A railing needs three reads at once — rhythm (posts), transparency (bars), finish (cap). Posts alone read as a fence line; bars alone read as a cage; uncapped posts read unfinished.
- **Recipe:** End posts + intermediate posts of **stone bricks**, 2 high, spaced 3–4 blocks apart. Between posts, **iron bars** 1 high spanning the top course (bars sit in the upper band, not full height). Cap every post with a **top-half slab** (stone-brick slab) as the handrail finish. Match the post material to the adjacent structure's stone family.
- **Apply when:** bridge edges, terrace/stair rails, plaza rims — anywhere people could "fall off" an edge. **NOT when:** technique 7's alternating high/low parapet already edges the same run — one railing language per edge, never both.
- **Prevents:** the floating-fence look (bars with no posts) and the prison look (bars with no rhythm or cap). (Pond ref: stone railing segment with iron-bar panels, foreground left.)

### 2. Land every railing on a full post *(extends technique 7)*

- **Principle:** A railing is a line with two ends; each end needs a visual full stop. A run that terminates mid-bar or mid-spacing reads as "ran out of materials," and on a bridge the landing is exactly where the eye checks for completion.
- **Recipe:** Every railing run ends in a full **2-high stone-brick post** standing on solid ground (or a 1×1 footing block where the ground is soft/uneven) — never terminate mid-bar, never end on a half-spacing. At a bridge end, the terminal post doubles as the landing marker: pair it with a **path block** (dirt path/grass path) continuing the walk line so the bridge visibly *arrives* somewhere.
- **Apply when:** any railing run, bridge ends especially. **NOT when:** the railing dies into a wall or pillar (the wall is the termination — no freestanding post needed).
- **Prevents:** railings that dissolve into grass mid-span; bridges that end in nothing. (Pond ref: the railing segment stands complete with finished end posts on the grass.)

### 3. The path-hub plaza *(extends technique 8: siting / grounds as architecture)*

- **Principle:** In any settlement with 3+ paths, the paths need a destination node — a designed center they converge on. Without it, paths wander and the settlement has no downtown; with it, every path has somewhere to *go*.
- **Recipe:** At the node where paths meet (or plan it first: lay paths to radiate from the chosen center): lay a pad **7–11 diameter** — circle of **stone slabs**, rimmed FIRST with a 1-wide border course of contrasting slabs (stone-brick slab), then filled inside (the rim is the layout tool: draw the circle with the rim, fill after). Set exactly **one anchor** (technique 10: tree, fountain, well, statue) dead center. Run each path INTO the rim — paths terminate at the pad edge, never cross the pad through the anchor. Rim contrast is mandatory: an unrimmed pad reads as a worn patch, not a plaza.
- **Apply when:** villages, town centers, courtyard hubs — anywhere ≥3 paths meet. **NOT when:** single-path builds (no hub needed); settlements already using technique 8's rectilinear cross-of-paths (pick one geometry per settlement — radial hub XOR formal cross).
- **Prevents:** path networks with no center; anchors marooned with no path logic; plazas that read as accidental clearings. (Pond ref: the tree plaza's circular rimmed pad as the platform's centerpiece; the video's narration places the pond/well at the town's path hub — same move, water variant.)

### 4. Give every anchor a clear radius *(extends technique 10: theme anchors)*

- **Principle:** An anchor justifies itself by dominating its surroundings. Props or structures crowding it compete for the eye and shrink it back to "one more object." Emptiness around the anchor is part of the anchor.
- **Recipe:** Keep a prop-free ring around every technique-10 anchor ≈ **1× the anchor's own width** (a 5-wide tree gets ~5 blocks of clear grass/pad around it). The ring may carry ground texture (tufts, path rim) but no vertical props, no secondary structures. If two anchors' radii would overlap, they're too close — move one or demote one to a regular prop.
- **Apply when:** any anchor — statues, centerpiece trees, fountains, oversized props. **NOT when:** the theme IS density (bazaar/market scenes run on technique 11's cluster grammar instead — clutter there is intentional, not competition).
- **Prevents:** the anchor disappearing into surrounding clutter; plazas that feel cramped despite being large. (Pond ref: the tree plaza stands isolated in open grass, ~15+ blocks from the nearest structure — the emptiness is doing work.)
