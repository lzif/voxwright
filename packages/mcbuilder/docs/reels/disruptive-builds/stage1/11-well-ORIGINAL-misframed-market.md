### 1. Striped stall canopy (extends technique 12)
- **Principle:** A small canopy reads as "market tent" the instant it wears stripes. The alternating 1-wide color bands are the whole identity — same construction in solid color reads as a shed roof.
- **Recipe:** 4 corner posts (dark oak logs or fences), 2–3 blocks tall; canopy frame of stairs sloping inward; canopy surface in alternating 1-wide stripes of white wool + ONE saturated accent wool (green, yellow, red, or blue — the frame shows green/white and yellow/white stalls). Canopy overhangs the stall footprint by 1 block on all sides. (Stripe block reads as wool; concrete would also work — flagged as inferred.)
- **Apply when:** market stalls, fair booths, dock kiosks, festival builds. **NOT when:** formal/symmetric builds (stripes read festive and fight solemnity); more than 2 colors per canopy (reads as circus, not market); canopies wider than ~5 blocks (stripes lose rhythm at large scale — switch to technique 12's pergola logic).
- **Prevents:** the "wooden box with stuff on it" stall that never reads as a shop. (Market ref)

### 2. Market stall as a complete unit (extends technique 11)
- **Principle:** A stall reads as a *shop* only when four parts are all present: counter + canopy + goods + one signifier (hanging lantern/sign). Missing the canopy it's a table; missing goods it's a tent; missing the signifier it's a shed.
- **Recipe (per stall, ~3×3 footprint):** counter — 2–3 wide base of barrels/crates with a top-half slab counter top; canopy per technique 1 above; goods ON the counter — 2–4 small groups (flower pots, 2-high crate stack, single barrel); one hanging lantern or sign from the canopy beam. Keep everything inside the stall footprint.
- **Apply when:** markets, bazaars, fairgrounds, dockside shops. **NOT when:** a lone stall with no market context (one stall in the wilderness reads as abandoned — stalls need the market to be a market).
- **Prevents:** prop confetti arranged around a table that never coheres into "shop". (Market ref)

### 3. Vary one variable per stall (extends technique 1)
- **Principle:** In a stall row, keep the construction grammar identical but change exactly ONE variable per stall — here, the canopy stripe accent color (green vs yellow) and the goods theme. Repetition-with-variation reads as a designed market; identical stalls read as copy-paste; fully different stalls read as clutter.
- **Recipe:** Same posts, same canopy shape, same counter build. Cycle the accent: stall A green/white stripes + flower goods, stall B yellow/white + crate goods, stall C red/white, etc. Goods theme follows the stripe color loosely (no strict rule needed — the color rhythm does the work).
- **Apply when:** any repeated small structure — market rows, village house clusters, dock kiosks, townhouse terraces. **NOT when:** the row should read as ONE complex (then unify the palette instead of varying).
- **Prevents:** copy-paste market rows on one side, visual noise on the other. (Market ref)

### 4. Stone slab market pad (extends technique 5)
- **Principle:** A defined ground plane tells the visitor "you have arrived" and visually contains the stalls. Without it, stalls sit on raw grass and read as dropped furniture.
- **Recipe:** Light stone-brick slabs, 1 block thick, footprint covering all stalls plus a ~2-block margin; edge slightly irregular — nibble corners off the rectangle so it reads as laid stone, not a platform. Path blocks (technique 5/8) should touch the pad edge, not stop 3 blocks short.
- **Apply when:** markets, plazas, fairgrounds, any multi-prop outdoor composition. **NOT when:** a single isolated prop (a pad for one barrel is a stage, not a ground plane).
- **Prevents:** stalls floating on undifferentiated grass; the "clean cutout" anti-pattern's cousin — props with no ground of their own. (Market ref)

### 5. Crate-and-barrel goods language (extends technique 7)
- **Principle:** "Wares" must read as merchandise, not storage. The difference is placement and scale: small stacks ON the counter (1–2 high, in the customer's sightline) read as goods for sale; the same crates stacked 3-high behind the stall read as stockroom.
- **Recipe:** On the counter: 2–4 item groups — a flower-pot cluster, one 2-high crate stack (barrels sideways or chiseled bookshelves), one upright barrel. Under/behind the counter: 1–2 barrels as stock, kept lower than the counter top. One open crate — trapdoor-framed box with visible contents — per stall as the "display piece."
- **Apply when:** shops, market stalls, dockside trading posts. **NOT when:** formal builds or the "abandoned" story (then scatter and tip the crates instead).
- **Prevents:** counters that read as storage shelves; goods that read as clutter. (Market ref)

### 6. Flower-box stall trim (extends technique 7's greenery box)
- **Principle:** A 2–3 pot strip of flowers along the stall's front edge is the cheapest "alive and trading" signal in the game — the same greenery-box verb as technique 7's window boxes, moved to retail.
- **Recipe:** 2–3 flower pots with red/yellow flowers in a row on the counter front edge or on a side crate. One color family per stall (matches the stall's accent).
- **Apply when:** any trading/leisure stall in temperate builds. **NOT when:** winter/arid themes — swap to dead bushes or cactus pots; the *strip* stays, the plant changes.
- **Prevents:** a fully stocked stall that still reads as closed/empty. (Market ref)
