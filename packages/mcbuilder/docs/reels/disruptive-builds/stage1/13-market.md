## Stage 1 — Pattern identification: town market (`~/workspace/disruptive-reel/frames/13-market.jpg`)

**Build observed:** a 3-stall market seed on a stone pad — each stall is 4 posts + striped fabric awning + display counter with colored goods + hanging lantern; the video's stated method is to repeat this module outward, varying accent colors and roof decorations per stall, to grow a town square.

### 1. The complete stall module (NEW)
- **Principle:** A market stall is a tiny building, not furniture — it needs all four subsystems (structure, roof, function, light). Drop any one and it reads as scaffolding (no awning), a bare table (no posts/awning), or a bus stop (no counter/goods).
- **Recipe (3×3 footprint):** 4 corner posts of stripped logs, 3 tall (4 for a "grand" stall). Awning at the top per technique 3 below. Counter: oak plank / bottom-half slab table, 2–3 wide × 1 deep, across the front at 1 block height, goods on top per technique 4. Light: 1 lantern hung centered under the awning's front beam per technique 6. Back and sides stay OPEN — a stall is entered from the front; walling it in turns it into a hut.
- **Apply when:** any commercial/retail vignette — markets, fairgrounds, dockside traders, village shops. **NOT when:** the "shop" needs lockable storage or interiors — then it's a shop *building* (techniques 2/7), not a stall.
- **Prevents:** the "table with a roof" failure — stalls that read as random furniture because a subsystem is missing. (Market ref)

### 2. Repeat-with-variation expansion: seed module → town square (NEW)
- **Principle:** This is the video's explicit method and the most transferable idea in the frame: design ONE good module, stamp it repeatedly, and vary a small set of accent parameters per instance. Identical repetition reads as copy-paste (a barracks, not a market); controlled variation on a fixed grammar reads as organic growth.
- **Recipe:** Fix the module grammar (footprint, post positions, awning pitch, counter position — identical on every instance). Vary per instance ONLY: (a) awning stripe colors — pick 2 from a fixed palette (white + one saturated color: green, red, blue, yellow); (b) counter goods colors (technique 4); (c) small roof decorations — flower pot or banner on the awning ridge; (d) lantern style (hanging vs post-mounted) — pick one per row. Vary 2–3 parameters per stall, never the module shape itself.
- **Apply when:** any multi-instance composition — market squares, village house rows, dock warehouses, festival grounds. **NOT when:** formal/symmetric architecture (use technique 8's enclosed plots); a single standalone instance (no repetition to vary).
- **Prevents:** the copy-paste curse — stamped modules that read as a cloning error instead of a grown place. (Market ref — the video states this method outright: "repeat these designs… change the different colored wall… and the decorations on top.")

### 3. Striped fabric awning construction (extends technique 7)
- **Principle:** Technique 7's awnings are wood/trapdoor shade planes. A market awning needs a different material language — *fabric* — and fabric reads via stripes. Alternating 1-block color stripes are what make wool read as canvas instead of a flat colored lid.
- **Recipe:** Canopy of wool (or concrete) in alternating 1-block-wide stripes, 2 colors, HIGH contrast (white + one saturated color — the striping vanishes at distance if the colors are close). Stripes run front-to-back (perpendicular to the counter) so the pattern reads from the approach. Slope: flat with the back edge 1 block higher than the front, or stepped via stairs; overhang the posts by 1 block on all sides; the canopy sits ON the posts — never floating. Scales by stripe count: the background's long black-and-white canopy is the same construction with more repeats.
- **Apply when:** stalls, cafes, festival tents, any "temporary fabric" read. **NOT when:** wet/nordic archetypes (fabric reads wrong — use plank awnings); formal stone architecture (use technique 7's porch scale); stripes wider than 1 block (reads as color-blocking, not awning).
- **Prevents:** wool canopies that read as flat painted lids; awnings floating off their posts. (Market ref)

### 4. Goods-as-color merchandising (NEW — relates to technique 11's theme-matching)
- **Principle:** At any render distance, merchandise reads as *color*, not detail. One saturated 1-block cube on a counter reads as "produce for sale" — no item frames, no fine detail needed. The goods' color IS the stall's theme signal (technique 11's theme-matching, applied to retail).
- **Recipe:** On each stall counter, place 1–2 full colored blocks (wool/concrete/terracotta in produce colors — yellow, red, green, orange), each on its own slab riser so it sits proud of the counter surface. Vary the goods color per stall (feeds technique 2's variation). Observed: yellow block (inferred: yellow wool/concrete or hay bale) and blue/purple block (inferred) on separate stalls.
- **Apply when:** any retail/display counter. **NOT when:** the counter is a workbench (tools, not produce — use technique 7's bits); more than 2–3 goods per counter (reads as storage, not display).
- **Prevents:** the empty-counter failure — a well-built stall with nothing to sell, which reads as abandoned. (Market ref — goods block identities inferred from color.)

### 5. Shared stall pad: zone the cluster (extends technique 5)
- **Principle:** Technique 5 grounds single buildings; a *cluster* needs shared ground. A paved pad under the stall group draws the market's boundary on the terrain — without it, stalls read as dropped on grass no matter how good each one is.
- **Recipe:** One continuous pad of stone-brick (or andesite) bottom-half slabs under the whole cluster, extending 2 blocks past the outermost stalls on all sides. The pad edge IS the market boundary — paths (technique 5/8) meet it, grass stays outside it.
- **Apply when:** any multi-structure cluster — markets, farmyards, workshop courts. **NOT when:** the pad would sit mostly empty (a plaza with 2 stalls reads as a parking lot — size the pad to the cluster, ~2 blocks of margin, no more); single builds (use technique 5's foundation instead).
- **Prevents:** "dropped on grass" syndrome — good stalls with no ground plane connecting them. (Market ref)

### 6. Stall-grid lantern rhythm (extends technique 6)
- **Principle:** Technique 6's core move is "light follows structure's rhythm." On a repeated module, the rhythm is the module grid itself: one identical lantern per stall, hung from the same point on each awning frame. The light grid and the stall grid become one read.
- **Recipe:** 1 lantern hung centered under each stall's awning front beam — same fixture, same position, every stall. At night the market reads as a dotted grid; by day the lanterns are the per-stall punctuation.
- **Apply when:** any repeated-module composition with per-module lighting. **NOT when:** a stall already carries a different light language (pick one system per technique 6); more than 1 lantern per stall (reads as a chandelier shop).
- **Prevents:** flat, evenly-lit market pads (the "well-lit parking lot" look) and random torch placement between stalls. (Market ref)

### 7. Face-the-walk: orient repeated modules to a shared spine (extends technique 11)
- **Principle:** Technique 11 stages arrival at one door; a market has N doors. The layout rule that makes N stalls read as one market: every counter faces the SAME shared circulation spine. Backs face outward, counters face the walk — that's what creates the "market street" instead of stalls facing random directions.
- **Recipe:** Lay the spine FIRST: a 2–3-wide path (dirt path or slabs) through the market area. Place stalls flanking it, counters facing the path, backs outward. Keep the full path width clear at all times (technique 11d — props flank, never block). When expanding (technique 2), extend the spine first, then flank the extension.
- **Apply when:** markets, food courts, bazaar streets, any repeated commercial frontage. **NOT when:** the modules are houses (fronts face varied directions for organic village feel — different rule); formal symmetric grounds (technique 8's axes already handle it).
- **Prevents:** "dead retail" — counters facing another stall's back, or facing open grass with no path to bring customers. (Market ref — all three counters face the pad's open center; the video's expansion method extends outward from this seed.)
